# Constructive Ordinals in Rocq

This is a Rocq development of ordinals built in constructive type theory.
Ordinals are a type of well-founded trees whose branching is indexed by an
arbitrary `Type`:

```coq
Inductive Ord : Type :=
  ord { ordCarrier :> Type
      ; ordSize :> ordCarrier -> Ord
      }.
```

The order relations `<`, `≤` and `≈` are defined by mutual recursion on this
structure. They can be read as a two-player game, where a constructive proof of
`x < y` is a winning strategy.

The development has two goals:

1. **A practical proof technique.** Ordinals serve as termination measures for
   recursive programs and as acyclicity arguments for complicated data
   structures. Through the coercions on `Ord`, a type can carry a canonical
   "size" measure, written `x ◃ y` (strictly smaller measure) and `x ⊴ y`.
2. **A climb into "Cantor's attic".** The development builds the Veblen
   hierarchy, the Feferman–Schütte ordinal Γ₀, the finite-tower Ackermann
   ordinal, the Ackermann ordinal, the small and large Veblen ordinals
   (SVO and LVO), and the Bachmann–Howard ordinal. It also gives ordinal
   notation systems up to each of these, with decidable ordering and
   constructive Cantor normal forms.

## Constructivity

All definitions are constructive. Every mathematical object is given
directly, usually as a recursive function. Proofs use the excluded middle only
where it is needed, or in a few cases where no constructive proof has been
found yet. Where it is used, it appears as an explicit hypothesis
(`EM : excluded_middle`) rather than as a global axiom. The same applies to
the dependent functional choice used in proofs about the infimum operator.
Most theorems should therefore report "Closed under the global context" under
`Print Assumptions`.

Many results that are classically unconditional instead take hypotheses that
ordinals are `complete`, meaning directed in a hereditary sense. `Classical.v`
shows that every ordinal is complete under the excluded middle, and that
several of these principles in turn imply (weak) excluded middle.

Reverse results of this kind appear throughout. For example, dropping the
completeness hypotheses for the closure of ω-powers under ordinary or natural
sums, or for Veblen fixpoints, implies weak excluded middle, and so does the
successor law for the natural product even on complete ordinals. Completeness itself is intensional: it is not preserved by
`≈` unless weak excluded middle holds (`complete_intensional_WEM`). Several of
the counterexamples were found by the Kripke-model search in `tools/kripke`.

The notation systems are well founded because of their semantic denotation
into `Ord`. Unlike the usual approach of the Schütte school, they need no
separate syntactic well-foundedness proof.

> **Exception:** `Countable.v` postulates bijections between `nat` and
> `nat + nat` / `nat * nat` with `Parameter`/`Axiom` declarations.

## Building

The project targets Rocq 9.x with the standard library (`From Stdlib ...`).
The `Makefile` is generated by `rocq makefile` from `_CoqProject`, which maps
the `Ordinal/` directory to the logical path `Ordinal`.

```sh
make                                          # build everything
rocq makefile -f _CoqProject -o Makefile      # regenerate the Makefile if needed
```

### Foundations

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `Defs.v` | The `Ord` type, `<`, `≤` and `≈`, and the game-theoretic reading of the order. Proves basic order facts, well-foundedness of `<`, ordinal and size induction, and setoid/morphism instances. Also a small `ordering`/comparison library used by the notation systems. |
| `Operators.v` | Basic constructions: zero, successor, limits (`limOrd`), binary and arbitrary suprema (`⊔`, `supOrd`), binary and infinitary infima (`glbOrd`, `infOrd`), predecessor, `boundedSup`, and ω. Defines *directed* and *complete* ordinals and *strongly continuous* functions. Characterizes zero, successor and limit ordinals. |
| `Arith.v` | Transfinite primitive recursion (`foldOrd`), and ordinal addition, multiplication and exponentiation with their algebraic laws. Also `powOmega` (ω^x), Knuth up-arrow functions on ordinals (including that `KnuthUp 2` produces ε-numbers), and embeddings of `nat`, `positive` and `N`. |
| `Enumerate.v` | `enumerates f P`, meaning `f` is the increasing enumeration of a class `P`. Includes uniqueness, zero and successor behaviour, and, under EM, surjectivity. Shows that surjectivity, left subtraction (`c ≤ z → ∃ a, c + a ≈ z`) and the converse "increasing and surjective implies enumerating" each imply EM. |
| `Fixpoints.v` | Least fixpoints above a base by ω-iteration (`fixOrd`), enumeration of the fixpoints of a normal function (`enum_fixpoints`), and the ε function. Proves that ε 0 is the least ω^x-closed ordinal and that `KnuthUp 2 ω ω ≈ ε 0`. |
| `Cantor.v` | Additively closed ordinals, Cantor sequences, and existence and uniqueness of Cantor normal form decompositions under EM. Shows that this decomposition theorem is equivalent to a classical principle, even when restricted to complete ordinals, and that additive closure of ω^x needs completeness (`expOmega_additively_closed_WEM`). |
| `NaturalArith.v` | Hessenberg natural (commutative) sum `naddOrd` and its relation to Cantor normal forms. Proves constructively that ω-powers of complete ordinals are closed under natural sums, and that natural and ordinary sums of a Cantor sequence agree when its steps are decided, with reverse results showing these hypotheses are needed; the natural sum does not preserve completeness. Also the `tree_selection` operator, which equals ω^x on complete ordinals and witnesses that completeness is intensional. Its least fixpoint is ε₀, constructively, but that every ε-number is one of its fixpoints implies WEM. Strong continuity of `tree_selection` implies WEM, so constructively Scott continuity does not imply strong continuity (`scott_strongly_continuous_WEM`). Includes a failed natural multiplication with a counterexample to distributivity (`nmul_not_distributive`), Jacobsthal multiplication, and "smash sums". |
| `NaturalProduct.v` | The natural (Hessenberg) product `nmulOrd`, defined as the rank of a game on finite unions of rectangles. Commutativity, monotonicity, zero and unit hold constructively; distributivity, the successor law and the exponent law ω^α ⊗ ω^β ≈ ω^(α ⊕ β) hold under EM, and `nmulOrd` agrees with Hessenberg's formula on Cantor normal forms. The lower halves of the successor law and of distributivity imply weak EM, even for complete ordinals. These reverse results transfer to any operation constructively ≈ to `nmulOrd`; no operation-independent version can rest on commutativity, monotonicity, zero, unit and the successor law alone, since the failed product of `NaturalArith.v` satisfies these constructively (`incorrect_product_laws`). On a universe of ordinals with a decidable order, superdistributivity holds constructively, and subdistributivity given a split of the natural sum (`decidable_universe`). |
| `NaturalExp.v` | There is no natural exponentiation: under EM, no operation `e` satisfies `e(α,1) = α`, weak monotonicity in each argument, `e(α, β ⊕ γ) = e(α,β) ⊗ e(α,γ)` and `e(α, β ⊗ γ) = e(e(α,β), γ)` (`natural_exp_impossible`). This is Altman's theorem (MLQ 2017, Theorem 4.1; independently Asperó and Tsaprounis 2018), with slightly weaker monotonicity hypotheses; the proof compares leading coefficients of ordinal-valued degrees and ends in a parity argument (`3^c ≠ 2^d`). EM is confined to an `e`-free lemma: constructively, the laws put `e(2,ω)` and `e(3,ω)` in a "⊗-logarithm ratio" of log₂ 3 (`laws_log23_ratio`), and only the impossibility of that ratio is classical (`no_log23_ratio`). Double-negated EM suffices (`natural_exp_impossible_nn`). In between sits the absence of *power families* (`f(2) ≥ ω`, monotone, `f(n^k) = f(n)^⊗k`): constructively, the ratio lemma rules them out (`power_family_ratio`) and the laws produce one (`laws_power_family`); this also gives Asperó and Tsaprounis's Theorem 3.2 (`aspero_tsaprounis`). |
| `Classical.v` | Consequences of excluded middle: totality and trichotomy, every ordinal is complete and directed, the classical well-ordering principle, and (with choice) that `infOrd` is attained. Also gives reverse results built on the "truth ordinal" of a proposition, showing that zero-testing, totality, order splitting, completeness, directedness and the successor/limit decision each imply (weak) excluded middle, and that if completeness is preserved by `≈` then EM holds (`complete_intensional_EM`). The truth ordinals embed the subobject classifier Ω as the trees `≤ 1`: up to `≈` these are exactly the propositions up to `↔` (`truth_ord_omega`), and the complete ones are exactly the decidable propositions, i.e. `0` and `1` (`truth_ord_complete_iff`, `complete_le_one`). |
| `Reflection.v` | The `reflects` predicate, which says that an operation on a syntactic type faithfully implements an operation on `Ord` (via a denotation and an invariant). The notation systems use it throughout. |

### Well-founded relations

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `WellFounded.v` | The ordinal of a well-founded relation (`wf_ord`), which depends only on the relation and not on the accessibility proof. Includes simulations and dominations between relations, and the transitive-closure construction that turns any ordinal back into a well-founded relation (`ord_R`). Computes the ordinals of standard constructions: inverse image, inclusion, transitive closure, the empty relation (0), successor, disjoint union (addition), symmetric product (smash sum / natural sum), lexicographic product (multiplication; `nat × nat` gives ω²), and lexicographic order on lists (exponentiation). Proves that the list-extension relation has the ordinal of the `tree_selection` operator (`NaturalArith.v`), hence ω^x classically. Read as Aczel sets, `wf_ord` is exactly the Mostowski collapse: constructively the unique solution of the collapse equation, injective on extensional relations and transitive-set-valued on transitive ones. So the round trip through `ord_R` (`vn_collapse`) is a von Neumann ordinal (a transitive set of transitive sets), constructively. Classically the von Neumann ordinals are exactly its fixed points and it identifies trees up to `≈` with them (`vn_collapse_quotient_classical`); but both `≈`-invariance (`vn_collapse_ext_EM`) and the fixed-point property (`vn_collapse_czf_fixed_EM`) imply EM. The transitive closure preserves completeness (`ord_transitive_closure_complete`). |

### Bachmann's hierarchy (`Bachmann*.v`)

Bachmann's hierarchy of normal functions φ_α, indexed by ordinals that may
exceed Ω, reaching the Bachmann–Howard ordinal. Indices are trees `BOrd` whose
nodes carry small children (treated by the Veblen "intersect" rule) and
Ω-families `C → Ord → BOrd` (treated by Bachmann's "diagonalize" rule); Ω is
the type `Ord` itself, so its regularity comes from universe stratification
rather than choice. Everything is constructive and axiom-free. The design
choices, and why a type of large ordinals is not enough, are explained in
[`BachmannNotes.md`](Ordinal/BachmannNotes.md).

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `BachmannDefs.v` | The trees `BOrd`, Bachmann's `bach` by structural recursion, the order `bsub`, the arithmetic on trees, the representation invariant `BRep`, and the general step theorems (enumeration, Ω-limit, successor, Veblen, +Ω). Agreement with the Veblen hierarchy below Ω. |
| `BachmannAssoc.v` | Tree addition is associative up to φ; the invariant `bfam` (every Ω-family is `bsub`-monotone). φ is monotone in `bsub` but not in the index order `ble`. |
| `BachmannPower.v` | β + Ω^ζ·ξ as the Schütte column 1+ζ, for every complete ζ (`bach_bPow_rep`); the canonical trees. |
| `BachmannCalibration.v` | φ against known hierarchies: φ_Ω = Γ, φ_{Ω+ξ} as a Veblen hierarchy over Γ, φ_{Ω·ξ} as ternary Veblen, φ_{Ω²}(0) = the Ackermann ordinal (`bach_Ωsq_Ackermann`), and the SVO and LVO (`bach_SVO_SmallVeblen`, `bach_LVO_LargeVeblen`). |
| `BachmannCfe.v` | The enumerator `cfe` of the common fixpoints of a normal function and a family of normal functions, and the hierarchy `bphi`, which applies it at each node and is normal by construction. |
| `BachmannNormal.v` | `bphi` is normal on canonical, decided trees (`bcanon`, `bgood`), including φ_{ε_{Ω+1}} (`bphi_εΩ1_normal`); calibrations of `bphi`. |
| `BachmannAgree.v` | Bachmann's one-component-at-a-time φ agrees with `bphi` under structural directedness (`bach_bphi_canon`), up to ε_{Ω+1}. Certifies the Bachmann–Howard ordinal: `BachmannHoward := bphi powOmega bεΩ1 0` and `bach_BachmannHoward`. |
| `BachmannFacts.v` | Semantic facts used by the notation system: identities between canonical trees up to `bsub`, the base 1+x, z-relative reachability, indices below Ω^Ω and Klammersymbols, dominance. |
| `BachmannEnumeration.v` | Facts about `cfe`/`bphi`: interpolation, comparing enumerators over different bases, merging equal exponents, and shifted enumerations. |

### Ordinal notation systems (`Notation/`)

Each system is an inductive type of terms (`VForm` or `CantorForm`). Each
provides a denotation into `Ord`, a decidable comparison proved correct, a
syntactic normal form with uniqueness, and a confluent, strongly normalizing
rewrite system. Each also provides a proof that the terms denote exactly the
target ordinal, and verified successor, addition, multiplication,
exponentiation, natural-sum and natural-product algorithms via the `reflects`
predicate. Under
EM, each system is shown to have "enough notations": every ordinal below a
notation is itself denoted by some notation.

| File | Ordinal reached | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| `CantorNotation.v` | ε₀ | Cantor normal forms (`CF ≈ CNF`, `CF < ε 0`), with direct addition, multiplication and exponentiation algorithms. |
| `Interpolants.v` | — | Interpolants: a constructive substitute for "every ordinal below a notation has a notation". Preserved by sums, fixpoints and Veblen hierarchies; with EM, having all interpolants is equivalent to having enough notations. |
| `CantorDecomposition.v` | — | Generic machinery. Any notation system with a verified Cantor decomposition (`has_cantor_decomposition`) gets successor, addition, multiplication and exponentiation algorithms for free, and a natural-sum algorithm that reflects `naddOrd` constructively. Shows that "has all interpolants" plus EM is equivalent to "has enough notations". |
| `CantorProduct.v` | — | The natural product on Cantor normal forms. The algorithm satisfies the usual laws constructively on notations, and, given interpolants (needed only at epsilon notations), reflects `nmulOrd` constructively. |
| `VNotation.v` | ε₀ | Binary Veblen-style terms `V a x` over 1+x (`VF ≈ ε 0`). |
| `VWNotation.v` | Γ₀ | Two-level `V`/`W` terms reflecting `veblen ε` (`VF ≈ Γ 0`). |
| `ATowerFinNotation.v` | Finite-tower Ackermann ordinal | Finite Ackermann towers (`VF ≈ FinAckermannOrdinal`). |
| `ATowerNotation.v` | Ackermann ordinal | Transfinite Ackermann towers (`VF ≈ AckermannOrdinal`). |
| `GrimmT3.v` | Ackermann ordinal | Grimm's syntactic presentation of Ackermann's 1951 system (from GAIA), translated into `ATowerNotation`. On normal forms Grimm's comparison agrees with the semantic order, and the translation is a bijection with the `ATowerNotation` normal forms (the inverse is `VF_to_T3`), so the normal forms have order type `AckermannOrdinal` (`T3_AO`) and inherit Cantor decomposition, interpolants, ordinary and natural arithmetic, and the impossibility of natural exponentiation. |
| `SchutteStack.v` | Small Veblen ordinal | Finitary Schütte stacks (`VF ≈ SmallVeblenOrdinal`). |
| `SchutteMatrix.v` | Large Veblen ordinal | Transfinite Schütte matrices (`VF ≈ LargeVeblenOrdinal`). |
| `BachmannNotation.v` | Bachmann–Howard ordinal | Terms `BN T y` (φ_T(y)) over Ω-Cantor normal form indices `Ω^e·c + r`; denotation via `bphi` at base 1+x, decidable comparison (`BS_compare_correct`) by Bachmann's nesting rule, and unique normal forms. The files below build on it. |
| `BachmannFragment.v` | Large Veblen ordinal | Indices below Ω^Ω embed Schütte's Klammersymbols (`Kl_correct`); the fragment has order type the LVO (`FragOrd_LVO`). |
| `BachmannCantor.v` | — | Cantor decomposition (`BS_has_cantor_decomposition`), hence successor, addition, multiplication, exponentiation and natural sum. |
| `BachmannBound.v` | — | Every normal form lies below BHO₁; coefficients lie below φ_T(0) (dominance). |
| `BachmannNormalize.v` | — | Confluent, strongly normalizing rewrite system on proper terms (`strongly_normalizing`, `reduce_confluent`), with normal forms exactly the irreducible terms. |
| `BachmannInterpolants.v` | — | Fundamental sequences as `bsub`-sandwiches and all interpolants (`BS_has_all_interpolants`); under EM, enough notations. |
| `BachmannOrderType.v` | Bachmann–Howard ordinal | The natural product (`BS_nmul_reflects`) and the order type of the normal forms (`BS_order_type`, `BS_order_type_BH`). |
| `BachmannAbsorb.v` | — | Offsets and absorption of a leading summand: φ_{Ω^e·c + R}(y) = φ_R(t + y). |
| `BachmannImproper.v` | Bachmann–Howard ordinal | Hereditary absorption (`hereditary_absorption`) and a normalizer `normSX` for *every* term, including improper indices and zero coefficients (`normalizeX_correct`, `normSX_complete`); every notation lies below BHO₁ (`BS_all_order_type_BH`). Improper terms are normalized by a function rather than a rewrite system. |

The `e`-free ratio lemma of `NaturalExp.v` holds constructively for notation-denoted ordinals (`no_log23_ratio_notation`). |

### Proof theory and termination (`Analysis/`)

Ordinal analyses: termination of hereditary substitution, strong normalization
of System T below ε₀, the consistency of Peano arithmetic, and, in the other
direction, the unprovability of Goodstein's theorem in Peano arithmetic and
of transfinite induction beyond ε₀.

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `HereditarySubst.v` | Hereditary substitution for simply-typed canonical terms in spine form, with termination by an ordinal measure: a type has ordinal ω·height, and a call at type τ measures |τ| + size. Termination holds on all inputs (`hsub_total`), the equations follow from determinism of the graph, and every measure is below ω·(height+1), hence below ω² (`measure_bound`). |
| `HereditarySubstLF.v` | The same for canonical LF: substitution into objects, spines, types and kinds, indexed by the simple-type erasure, which substitution preserves (`erase_hsub_tp`). The ordinal measure and its bound carry over unchanged (`lmeasure_bound`). |
| `HereditarySubstF.v` | An ordinal measure for Leivant's predicative System F: the kinds of a type become the natural sum of ω^k over its quantifiers (`kord`), substitution adds one copy of the instance per occurrence (`kord_tsubst`), and since ω^k is closed under natural sums, instantiating a quantifier with a well-kinded type decreases the measure ω·κ + depth (`tord_inst`). A type of kind k has measure below ω^(k+1), so all measures are below ω^ω (`tord_bound`). |
| `HereditarySubstFNorm.v` | Hereditary substitution for predicative System F: terminating on all inputs by the ordinal measure, with a decidable kind check at type-application redexes (`hsub_total`); type-preserving (`HS_typed`) and correct with respect to β-reduction (`HS_red`) on well-typed input. Hence every well-typed term reduces to a normal form of the same type (`normalization_red`), and ∀X:0. X is uninhabited (`consistency`). |
| `SystemT.v` | An ordinal analysis of Gödel's System T below ε₀, after Tait: the recursor becomes an ω-branching term, and derivations of infinitary terms carry a cut rank and a height that is a V form (`VNotation`). A term t translates at height `h t` < ω^ω and cut rank `tlev t` (`tr_typed`, `h_below_ω_ω`); one pass of cut elimination lowers the cut rank by one and sends height α to ω^α (`elim_typed`), so the normal form has height `ord_of t`, a tower of `tlev t` exponentials over `h t`, so below ω_{tlev t + 2} (`T_normalization`, `ord_of_bound`). Closed terms of type nat reduce to numerals (`T_total`). |
| `SystemTCL.v` | Strong normalization of combinatory System T (K, S, recursor, pairs) by a Howard–Schütte style ordinal assignment below ε₀. Each term gets a vector of normal V forms indexed by type levels, built with Howard's operation ω^(next level) ⊗ (f ⊕ g); every reduction step lowers the vector (`step_decreases`, with Howard's lemma for S in `S_decrease` and the recursor in `R_decrease`), so typed terms are strongly normalizing (`typed_SN`). Confluence by complete developments (`confluence`); every closed term of type N reduces to a unique numeral (`T_value`). The recursor reduces on numeral arguments, as in Howard (1970); projections reduce like K. |
| `PeanoArith.v` | Peano and Heyting arithmetic in natural deduction (de Bruijn variables, `0`, `S`, `+`, `·`, and a unary predicate variable `X` with no axioms), with the induction rule and the derived induction axiom (`induction_axiom`), closure under substitution (`prov_subst`), and the Gödel–Gentzen negative translation of PA into HA (`negative_translation`, `PA_HA_consistency`). |
| `Dialectica.v` | Consistency of Peano arithmetic (`PA_consistent`) by Gödel's Dialectica interpretation into `SystemTCL.v`. Each formula gets witness and counterexample types and a quantifier-free matrix, a term of type N that is true when it reduces to 0, read on closed instances. Every HA derivation yields T terms realizing it (`HA_sound`); contraction merges counterexamples by deciding the matrices, and induction uses the recursor. Both steps rest on `T_value` (every closed term of type N has a unique numeral value), so on the ε₀ strong-normalization proof. The predicate variable `X` is read as the empty set. |
| `Hardy.v` | The Hardy hierarchy below ε₀ on V forms (not necessarily normal): the predecessors P_n of the standard fundamental sequences, the relation β ≼_n α (β reached from α by P_n steps) and H_α, as inductive relations. Proves the Bachmann property P_n α ≼_{n+1} P_{n+1} α (`Pn_bachmann`), that H_α is increasing and monotone along ≼_n (`hardy_mono`), H_{γ+δ} = H_γ ∘ H_δ (`hardy_vcat`), and that for normal forms α < β gives α ≼_n β once n exceeds the size of α (`reach_of_lt`, `hardy_dominated`). |
| `Cichon.v` | Cichon's theorem: subtracting one in hereditary base b is P_b on the ordinal (`hbn_dec_Pn`), so the Goodstein sequence from m first reaches 0 at step k exactly when H_{o(m)}(3) = k + 3, where o(m) is the ordinal of m in hereditary base 2 (`cichon`). |
| `OmegaLogic.v` | A Tait-style ω-logic for arithmetic with Buchholz–Wainer controls: derivations carry a control n, heights related by ≺_n, and a rule raising the control to m ≤ H_γ(n) at the cost of γ. Bounded formulas are decided and treated as atoms; `X`-atoms have identity axioms. Cut elimination costs one exponential per cut rank (`od_elim`, `od_elim_all`), and a cut-free derivation of a Σ₁ sentence at height α and control n has a witness below H_α(n) (`od_bounding`, `od_witness`). |
| `Embedding.v` | Every PA derivation becomes, for each assignment of numerals below the control, an ω-derivation of height ω³·k and fixed cut rank (`embed`); term values are bounded by H_{ω²·k} (`term_bound`). |
| `GoodsteinIndependence.v` | Every Σ₁ sentence ∀x∃y θ provable in PA, θ bounded, has witnesses below H_α(x+1) for a fixed α < ε₀ (`PA_sigma1_bound`). Against Cichon's lower bound (`goodstein_diagonal`) this gives the unprovability of any bounded formulation of Goodstein's theorem (`goodstein_unprovable`), without Gödel's incompleteness theorem. |
| `GoodsteinFormula.v` | Goodstein's theorem as a sentence of arithmetic (`goodstein_sentence`): θ(x,y) checks a certificate coded with Gödel's β-function (`beta_exists`, by the Chinese remainder theorem), consisting of the sequence and, for each step, tables of powers and of the hereditary base change, checked through leading terms. The sentence is true (`goodstein_sentence_true`) and not provable in PA (`goodstein_independent`). |
| `ProvableTI.v` | Peano arithmetic proves transfinite induction only below ε₀ (the upper half of "the proof-theoretic ordinal of PA is ε₀"). If PA proves TI(≺, X) for a relation ≺ given by a bounded formula, then ≺ has height below ε₀ (`PA_TI_bound`): every element is classically below a fixed L < ε₀. By Schütte's boundedness argument on the ω-logic: in a derivation of height α with cuts on bounded formulas only, the X-positive part of a sequent ¬Prog(X), Δ holds when X is read as "below γ + ω^α" (`od_TI_bound`). Since PA is classical the conclusion is a classical ("not not") rank bound, the faithful constructive reading of PA's proof; it excludes infinite descending sequences (`PA_TI_no_descent`). As a classical corollary, with the excluded middle as an explicit hypothesis, ≺ is well-founded of order type below ε₀ (`PA_TI_wf_EM`). |

## Lean port

`lean/` contains a complete port of the development (except `Countable.v`) to
Lean 4, using only Lean core and no axioms: no declaration depends on
`propext`, `Quot.sound` or `Classical.choice`, and the excluded middle
appears only as an explicit hypothesis, as in Rocq. All 3,847 Rocq
declarations outside `Countable.v` are accounted for, and a generated
statement review sets 470 headline statements beside their Rocq originals.
See `lean/README.md` for how to build and check it, and for the differences
from Rocq.

## Tools

`tools/kripke` contains Python tools that search two-world Kripke models for
counterexamples to constructive versions of ordinal principles. Several
reverse results in this development were found with them. `tools/kripke/README.md`
explains how to rerun the searches behind each result.
