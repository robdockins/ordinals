# Constructive Ordinals in Rocq

This is a Rocq development of ordinals built in constructive type theory.
Ordinals are a type of well-founded trees whose branching is indexed by an
arbitrary `Type`:

```coq
Inductive Ord : Type :=
  ord { ordCarrier :> Type
      ; ordSize :> ordCarrier -> Ord
      }.
```

The order relations `<`, `≤` and `≈` are defined by mutual recursion on this
structure. They can be read as a two-player game, where a constructive proof of
`x < y` is a winning strategy.

The development has two goals:

1. **A practical proof technique.** Ordinals serve as termination measures for
   recursive programs and as acyclicity arguments for complicated data
   structures. Through the coercions on `Ord`, a type can carry a canonical
   "size" measure, written `x ◃ y` (strictly smaller measure) and `x ⊴ y`.
2. **A climb into "Cantor's attic".** The development builds the Veblen
   hierarchy, the Feferman–Schütte ordinal Γ₀, the finite-tower Ackermann
   ordinal, the Ackermann ordinal, and the small and large Veblen ordinals
   (SVO and LVO). It also gives ordinal notation systems up to each of these,
   with decidable ordering and constructive Cantor normal forms.

## Constructivity

All definitions are constructive. Every mathematical object is given
directly, usually as a recursive function. Proofs use the excluded middle only
where it is needed, or in a few cases where no constructive proof has been
found yet. Where it is used, it appears as an explicit hypothesis
(`EM : excluded_middle`) rather than as a global axiom. The same applies to
the dependent functional choice used in proofs about the infimum operator.
Most theorems should therefore report "Closed under the global context" under
`Print Assumptions`.

Many results that are classically unconditional instead take hypotheses that
ordinals are `complete`, meaning directed in a hereditary sense. `Classical.v`
shows that every ordinal is complete under the excluded middle, and that
several of these principles in turn imply (weak) excluded middle.

Reverse results of this kind appear throughout. For example, dropping the
completeness hypotheses for the closure of ω-powers under ordinary or natural
sums, or for Veblen fixpoints, implies weak excluded middle, and so does the
successor law for the natural product even on complete ordinals. Completeness itself is intensional: it is not preserved by
`≈` unless weak excluded middle holds (`complete_intensional_WEM`). Several of
the counterexamples were found by the Kripke-model search in `tools/kripke`.

The notation systems are well founded because of their semantic denotation
into `Ord`. Unlike the usual approach of the Schütte school, they need no
separate syntactic well-foundedness proof.

> **Exception:** `Countable.v` postulates bijections between `nat` and
> `nat + nat` / `nat * nat` with `Parameter`/`Axiom` declarations.

## Building

The project targets Rocq 9.x with the standard library (`From Stdlib ...`).
The `Makefile` is generated by `rocq makefile` from `_CoqProject`, which maps
the `Ordinal/` directory to the logical path `Ordinal`.

```sh
make                                          # build everything
rocq makefile -f _CoqProject -o Makefile      # regenerate the Makefile if needed
```

## Contents

Files are listed roughly in dependency order. All paths are relative to
`Ordinal/`.

### Foundations

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `Defs.v` | The `Ord` type, `<`, `≤` and `≈`, and the game-theoretic reading of the order. Proves basic order facts, well-foundedness of `<`, ordinal and size induction, and setoid/morphism instances. Also a small `ordering`/comparison library used by the notation systems. |
| `Operators.v` | Basic constructions: zero, successor, limits (`limOrd`), binary and arbitrary suprema (`⊔`, `supOrd`), binary and infinitary infima (`glbOrd`, `infOrd`), predecessor, `boundedSup`, and ω. Defines *directed* and *complete* ordinals and *strongly continuous* functions. Characterizes zero, successor and limit ordinals. |
| `Arith.v` | Transfinite primitive recursion (`foldOrd`), and ordinal addition, multiplication and exponentiation with their algebraic laws. Also `powOmega` (ω^x), Knuth up-arrow functions on ordinals (including that `KnuthUp 2` produces ε-numbers), and embeddings of `nat`, `positive` and `N`. |
| `Enumerate.v` | `enumerates f P`, meaning `f` is the increasing enumeration of a class `P`. Includes uniqueness, zero and successor behaviour, and, under EM, surjectivity. Shows that surjectivity, left subtraction (`c ≤ z → ∃ a, c + a ≈ z`) and the converse "increasing and surjective implies enumerating" each imply EM. |
| `Fixpoints.v` | Least fixpoints above a base by ω-iteration (`fixOrd`), enumeration of the fixpoints of a normal function (`enum_fixpoints`), and the ε function. Proves that ε 0 is the least ω^x-closed ordinal and that `KnuthUp 2 ω ω ≈ ε 0`. |
| `Cantor.v` | Additively closed ordinals, Cantor sequences, and existence and uniqueness of Cantor normal form decompositions under EM. Shows that this decomposition theorem is equivalent to a classical principle, even when restricted to complete ordinals, and that additive closure of ω^x needs completeness (`expOmega_additively_closed_WEM`). |
| `NaturalArith.v` | Hessenberg natural (commutative) sum `naddOrd` and its relation to Cantor normal forms. Proves constructively that ω-powers of complete ordinals are closed under natural sums, and that natural and ordinary sums of a Cantor sequence agree when its steps are decided, with reverse results showing these hypotheses are needed; the natural sum does not preserve completeness. Also the `tree_selection` operator, which equals ω^x on complete ordinals and witnesses that completeness is intensional. Its least fixpoint is ε₀, constructively, but that every ε-number is one of its fixpoints implies WEM. Includes a failed natural multiplication with a counterexample to distributivity (`nmul_not_distributive`), Jacobsthal multiplication, and "smash sums". |
| `NaturalProduct.v` | The natural (Hessenberg) product `nmulOrd`, defined as the rank of a game on finite unions of rectangles. Commutativity, monotonicity, zero and unit hold constructively; distributivity, the successor law and the exponent law ω^α ⊗ ω^β ≈ ω^(α ⊕ β) hold under EM, and `nmulOrd` agrees with Hessenberg's formula on Cantor normal forms. The lower halves of the successor law and of distributivity imply weak EM, even for complete ordinals. On a universe of ordinals with a decidable order, superdistributivity holds constructively, and subdistributivity given a split of the natural sum (`decidable_universe`). |
| `NaturalExp.v` | There is no natural exponentiation: under EM, no operation `e` satisfies `e(α,1) = α`, weak monotonicity in each argument, `e(α, β ⊕ γ) = e(α,β) ⊗ e(α,γ)` and `e(α, β ⊗ γ) = e(e(α,β), γ)` (`natural_exp_impossible`). This is Altman's theorem (MLQ 2017, Theorem 4.1; independently Asperó and Tsaprounis 2018), with slightly weaker monotonicity hypotheses; the proof compares leading coefficients of ordinal-valued degrees and ends in a parity argument (`3^c ≠ 2^d`). EM is confined to an `e`-free lemma: constructively, the laws put `e(2,ω)` and `e(3,ω)` in a "⊗-logarithm ratio" of log₂ 3 (`laws_log23_ratio`), and only the impossibility of that ratio is classical (`no_log23_ratio`). Double-negated EM suffices (`natural_exp_impossible_nn`). In between sits the absence of *power families* (`f(2) ≥ ω`, monotone, `f(n^k) = f(n)^⊗k`): constructively, the ratio lemma rules them out (`power_family_ratio`) and the laws produce one (`laws_power_family`); this also gives Asperó and Tsaprounis's Theorem 3.2 (`aspero_tsaprounis`). |
| `Classical.v` | Consequences of excluded middle: totality and trichotomy, every ordinal is complete and directed, the classical well-ordering principle, and (with choice) that `infOrd` is attained. Also gives reverse results built on the "truth ordinal" of a proposition, showing that zero-testing, totality, order splitting, completeness, directedness and the successor/limit decision each imply (weak) excluded middle, and that if completeness is preserved by `≈` then EM holds (`complete_intensional_EM`). |
| `Reflection.v` | The `reflects` predicate, which says that an operation on a syntactic type faithfully implements an operation on `Ord` (via a denotation and an invariant). The notation systems use it throughout. |

### Well-founded relations

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `WellFounded.v` | The ordinal of a well-founded relation (`wf_ord`), which depends only on the relation and not on the accessibility proof. Includes simulations and dominations between relations, and the transitive-closure construction that turns any ordinal back into a well-founded relation (`ord_R`). Computes the ordinals of standard constructions: inverse image, inclusion, transitive closure, the empty relation (0), successor, disjoint union (addition), symmetric product (smash sum / natural sum), lexicographic product (multiplication; `nat × nat` gives ω²), and lexicographic order on lists (exponentiation). Proves that the list-extension relation has the ordinal of the `tree_selection` operator (`NaturalArith.v`), hence ω^x classically. Read as an Aczel set, the round trip through `ord_R` is the Mostowski collapse of the transitive closure: classically it maps `≈`-equivalent trees to extensionally equal sets (the von Neumann ordinal), but that invariance implies EM (`vn_collapse_ext_EM`). The transitive closure preserves completeness (`ord_transitive_closure_complete`). |

### The Veblen hierarchy and beyond

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `VeblenDefs.v` | The Veblen hierarchy `veblen f β x` over a base function `f`, and the Γ function (enumerating the strongly critical ordinals). |
| `VeblenCon.v` | The constructive theory of normal functions and the Veblen hierarchy, assuming completeness instead of EM. Shows that `veblen f β` enumerates the β-critical points. Γ is normal and enumerates the strongly critical ordinals of ω^x, and Γ₀ is the least fixpoint of `β ↦ φ_β(0)`. Reverse results show that the completeness hypotheses for `veblen` being increasing and for its fixpoints are needed. |
| `VeblenClassical.v` | A parallel, classical (EM-based) development of the same results. It goes one level further with Ξ, which enumerates the strongly critical ordinals of Γ. It is standalone; nothing imports it. |
| `VeblenFacts.v` | Further facts about Veblen functions, especially over the base `1 + x` (`veblen_onePlus`). The Ackermann and Schütte material uses these. |
| `AckermannTower.v` | Ackermann-style towers of Veblen functions (`ackermann_tower`) and a finite-height variant. Proves normality and fixpoint properties, and defines the `FinAckermannOrdinal` and `AckermannOrdinal`. |
| `SchutteColumn.v` | Schütte's "Klammersymbol" functions in column form (`schutte_column f β α x`), which generalize Veblen functions to more arguments, and their monotonicity, continuity and normality. Defines `E_number`. |
| `SchutteColumnFacts.v` | Critical, supercritical, hypercritical and unreachable ordinals for Schütte functions. Defines the `SmallVeblenOrdinal` and `LargeVeblenOrdinal`. Also defines finitary Schütte *stacks* and transfinite Schütte *matrices*, with the enumeration, normal-form and lexicographic comparison results that the SVO and LVO notation systems use. |

### Ordinal notation systems (`Notation/`)

Each system is an inductive type of terms (`VForm` or `CantorForm`). Each
provides a denotation into `Ord`, a decidable comparison proved correct, a
syntactic normal form with uniqueness, and a confluent, strongly normalizing
rewrite system. Each also provides a proof that the terms denote exactly the
target ordinal, and verified successor, addition, multiplication,
exponentiation, natural-sum and natural-product algorithms via the `reflects`
predicate. Under
EM, each system is shown to have "enough notations": every ordinal below a
notation is itself denoted by some notation.

| File | Ordinal reached | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| `CantorNotation.v` | ε₀ | Cantor normal forms (`CF ≈ CNF`, `CF < ε 0`), with direct addition, multiplication and exponentiation algorithms. |
| `Interpolants.v` | — | Interpolants: a constructive substitute for "every ordinal below a notation has a notation". Preserved by sums, fixpoints and Veblen hierarchies; with EM, having all interpolants is equivalent to having enough notations. |
| `CantorDecomposition.v` | — | Generic machinery. Any notation system with a verified Cantor decomposition (`has_cantor_decomposition`) gets successor, addition, multiplication and exponentiation algorithms for free, and a natural-sum algorithm that reflects `naddOrd` constructively. Shows that "has all interpolants" plus EM is equivalent to "has enough notations". |
| `CantorProduct.v` | — | The natural product on Cantor normal forms. The algorithm satisfies the usual laws constructively on notations, and, given interpolants (needed only at epsilon notations), reflects `nmulOrd` constructively. |
| `VNotation.v` | ε₀ | Binary Veblen-style terms `V a x` over 1+x (`VF ≈ ε 0`). |
| `VWNotation.v` | Γ₀ | Two-level `V`/`W` terms reflecting `veblen ε` (`VF ≈ Γ 0`). |
| `ATowerFinNotation.v` | Finite-tower Ackermann ordinal | Finite Ackermann towers (`VF ≈ FinAckermannOrdinal`). |
| `ATowerNotation.v` | Ackermann ordinal | Transfinite Ackermann towers (`VF ≈ AckermannOrdinal`). |
| `GrimmT3.v` | Ackermann ordinal | Grimm's syntactic presentation of Ackermann's 1951 system (from GAIA), translated into `ATowerNotation`. On normal forms Grimm's comparison agrees with the semantic order, and the normal forms have order type `AckermannOrdinal` (`T3_AO`). |
| `SchutteStack.v` | Small Veblen ordinal | Finitary Schütte stacks (`VF ≈ SmallVeblenOrdinal`). |
| `SchutteMatrix.v` | Large Veblen ordinal | Transfinite Schütte matrices (`VF ≈ LargeVeblenOrdinal`). |
| `NaturalExpNotation.v` | — | No natural exponentiation on notation systems, constructively: for any notation system with a Cantor decomposition and interpolants (all of the above), no operation on its notations satisfies the laws of a natural exponentiation restricted to them (`natural_exp_impossible_notation`; e.g. `natural_exp_impossible_schutte_matrix`). The `e`-free ratio lemma of `NaturalExp.v` holds constructively for notation-denoted ordinals (`no_log23_ratio_notation`). |

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|

## Lean port

`lean/` contains a complete port of the development (except `Countable.v`) to
Lean 4, using only Lean core and no axioms: no declaration depends on
`propext`, `Quot.sound` or `Classical.choice`, and the excluded middle
appears only as an explicit hypothesis, as in Rocq. All 2,439 Rocq
declarations outside `Countable.v` are accounted for, and a generated
statement review sets 360 headline statements beside their Rocq originals.
See `lean/README.md` for how to build and check it, and for the differences
from Rocq.

## Tools

`tools/kripke` contains Python tools that search two-world Kripke models for
counterexamples to constructive versions of ordinal principles. Several
reverse results in this development were found with them. `tools/kripke/README.md`
explains how to rerun the searches behind each result.
